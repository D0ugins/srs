# CMU Buggy — Overview

This document grounds an agent in the CMU Buggy domain in one file. It is deliberately breadth-first: every claim that a downstream system might act on carries a source and reliability tier, and the caveats that guard against confident misuse are kept even where they interrupt the prose. Reliability tiers used throughout: **R1** = official record (the rulebook and the canon.md race results, ground truth); **R2** = well-curated secondary reference (cmubuggy.org prose, team wikis, near-verbatim bylaw copies); **R3** = informal (chat, podcasts, alumni recollection, memes) — vivid but memory-drifted and, in some channels, speculative by design.

## 1. What Sweepstakes / Buggy is

Buggy — formally **Sweepstakes** — is a relay race held annually at Carnegie Mellon University since **1920**, run during **Spring Carnival** in mid-April [web:reference/overview|R2]. It is one of the oldest continuous traditions at CMU, with 100-plus years of history broken only by the WWII years (1942–1945) and the 2020 COVID cancellation [canon|R1]. A team of **five pushers** relay-pushes an unmotorized composite vehicle — **the buggy** — that carries one small **driver** around a roughly **0.8-mile** loop clockwise around Flagstaff Hill in Schenley Park [web:reference/course|R2][web:reference/overview|R2]. The buggy is the relay "baton": as it moves through the course, control passes from pusher to pusher, and on the downhill from the pushers to the driver, who rides sealed inside and steers.

Roughly half the course is uphill, where pushers run and shove, and half is a downhill **freeroll** where no one pushes and the driver alone controls the buggy at up to roughly 35–40 mph [web:reference/course|R2]. (The exact top speed is unsettled in the corpus: older Robobuggy recruiting material says "up to 35 mph," while SRS's 2025 raceday seminar says "up to 40 mph"; treat any single top-speed figure as approximate [drive:robobuggy/RD24/GBM|R3][drive:srs/Raceday '25 Seminar|R2].) Over 400 students across roughly 11–15 organizations participate each year, about half of them Greek and half independent; the 2026 field was 11 teams — 10 human-pushed plus one robotic [web:reference/overview|R2][pod:rd26_preview|R3].

A plain-language description used in SRS sponsorship materials captures the sport well: student organizations "build a non-motorized rolling torpedo thingy (the buggy) then put a VERY small person in it (the driver) and push them around a course like a baton in a relay race" [drive:srs/RD22 Nucor Draft|R3]. What began (per team lore) as fraternity members racing wheeled barrels down a hill has become, in the community's own words, a "hyper-engineered, televised annual race" — SRS brands it a "Freeroll Racing Sport" [pod:s06e01|R3][drive:srs/RD20 GBM|R3]. **Spring Carnival** itself is CMU's largest annual campus event, and Buggy is its marquee athletic tradition; the race draws alumni back each year, and the BAA (below) livestreams and broadcasts it.

**Race format.** Racing happens over Spring Carnival weekend, roughly Thursday through Saturday. **Thursday** is the **Design Competition** (now often called Showcase), where buggies are judged on engineering and construction. **Friday** holds the **preliminary** time-trial races — all teams roll, narrowing to roughly the top ten per class. **Saturday** holds exhibition rolls and the **finals** for the best overall time, followed by the alumni meeting, awards, and a barbecue [news:2024/09/buggy-course-tour|R2][drive:srs/Raceday '25 Seminar|R2]. Teams race in grouped **heats** rather than all at once; a heat-selection process (introduced 1973) keeps top contenders from being paired dangerously [news:2019/12|R2]. Because a disqualified run still records a time but earns no placing, "fastest time" and "winner" are not always the same thing.

**Classes.** There are four: **Men's**, **Women's** (raced since 1979), **All Gender** (official results from 2023), and **Robotic** (autonomous or radio-controlled, driverless buggies) [canon|R1]. Each organization may field multiple entries per class, labeled **A/B/C/D**, with A the most competitive; entries within a class must race fastest-to-slowest by that label [web:official-rules|R1].

**Season and calendar.** A buggy "season" spans the prior fall through spring Raceday, and seasons are numbered from 1920 (2023–24 was "Year 103") [news:2023/09|R2]. The bulk of the year is not racing but **practice**. **Rolls** (also called freerolls) are weekend dawn practices, from sunrise to about 9am, in the fall (late September to mid-November) and spring (mid-February to Raceday), cancelled for rain, snow, or unsafe roads [web:reference/freerolls|R2]. **Midnights** (formally "Push Practice") are late-night, hill-specific pusher-only sessions running from about spring break to Raceday [drive:srs/RD22 Seminar|R3]. A **Mini Raceday** in November and a **Truck Weekend** near Raceday serve as scaled practice and heat-seeding events. Note a naming convention that recurs across the corpus: **RD26 = Raceday 2026**, and SRS Drive folders named "Raceday '25/'26/'27" denote that year's build-and-season cycle, work on which often begins the prior fall [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §4].

## 2. The course

The course is a loop of roughly **0.8 mile** run clockwise around Flagstaff Hill in Schenley Park, on public roads shared with the city [web:reference/course|R2]. Per the official bylaws it starts near the Margaret Morrison / Tech Street intersection ("Maggie Mo"), runs south down Tech Street, west along Schenley Drive (past the Westinghouse Memorial Pond and the Panther Hollow bridge, and between the Bigelow monument and Phipps Conservatory), turns onto Frew Street through "the chute," and finishes on Frew near the GSIA / Tepper building [web:official-rules|R1][web:reference/course|R2].

**Course-length caveat (keep this).** The R1 rules define the course by street geometry and per-segment distances **in feet** and never state an aggregate mileage, so the commonly cited figure cannot be resolved from the official record. Sources give **"approximately 0.8 miles (also cited as 0.84 miles)"** with total elevation gain about 85 ft but net gain only 24 ft [web:reference/course|R2]. The primer and several SRS seminar decks use 0.84 mi; the reference page and 2024 course tour use 0.8 mi. Treat **"roughly 0.8 mile"** as the safe statement and do not present either figure as exact [see topics/course.md].

**Six segments, in race order.** The course comprises five pushed hills and, between them, an unpushed downhill and its turn:

- **Hill 1** (push, from a dead stop) — from the bottom of Tech Street up to where Tech meets Frew; the only hill where the buggy starts stationary, demanding explosive power on the steepest sustained grade. Roughly 365 ft / 111 m with the greatest elevation change (~27 ft) [web:reference/course|R2][wiki:mvp:pusher|R2]. The fastest Hill 1 pushers each Raceday, by class, are crowned the **Blinn/Brewer King, Queen, or Monarch of the Hill** [pod:s06e04|R3]. Weight matters most here: a heavy buggy is a Hill 1 liability.
- **Hill 2** (push, short and technical) — the shortest hill (~211 ft / 64 m). The Hill 2 pusher's final shove, timed while the driver is aimed straight downhill, largely sets **freeroll entry speed**, which is why several sources call it the most important hill for speed [pod:s01e01|R3][wiki:mvp:pusher|R2].
- **Freeroll** (no push) — the mostly-downhill section where the buggy outruns the pushers and rolls freely, reaching up to ~40 mph [web:reference/course|R2]. Only very light or heavily-ballasted buggies (robotic buggies, practice buggies with weight bags) ever need a push after the top of Hill 2.
- **Chute** — the sharp **right turn** from Schenley Drive onto Frew Street at the bottom of the freeroll, taken at maximum speed. This is the sharpest, fastest turn and the primary crash site [web:reference/course|R2]. SRS measures a characteristic turn radius of about **124 ft taken near 27 mph**, though other teams' estimates and assumptions range 100–130 ft, each derived differently — the exact radius is not settled [wiki:research:buggy_vehicle_dynamics|R2][see topics/course.md]. (Terminology note: an alum argues that strictly the "chute" is the steep section between the flags and the "turn" is the section after it, and that the two have been conflated for years [news:2009/10|R3].)
- **Rollout** — the distance a buggy coasts past the chute before the Hill 3 pusher picks it up, a rough proxy for mechanical and driving efficiency. There is no scale on the course, so rollout is measured informally in Porter Hall **"windows"** (now parking spaces); **Window 0** is the Porter/Scaife driveway edge, "rolling the plug" reaches a fire hydrant far down, and "rolling the gazebo" (stopping at the Frew/Schenley corner) is never good [web:reference/course|R2].
- **Hills 3, 4, 5** (push, the "back hills") — Hill 3 is steepest and shortest (~233 ft / 71 m), favoring strength and the one significant **left** turn onto Frew; Hill 4 (~404 ft / 123 m) changes grade dramatically; Hill 5 (~544 ft / 166 m) is the longest and flattest, a roughly 150 m sprint to the finish [web:reference/course|R2][wiki:mvp:pusher|R2]. "Front hills" = 1–2, "back hills" = 3–5. Between hills are three 45-foot **transition zones** (Hill 1–2, 3–4, 4–5) where two pushers may both legally touch the buggy [web:official-rules|R1].

**Hill-length caveat.** The per-hill figures above are R2 team-wiki values from 2022 and conflict with other readings by 5–15% (e.g. Hill 5 is variously 544 ft / 166 m, "165 m official," ~160 m, or ~150 m) [see topics/course.md]. Use them as shape, not survey. Team-measured segment distances exist too (SRS "Raceday Breakdowns": crosswalk-to-stop-sign ~1248 ft, stop-sign-to-transition-flag ~312 ft, transition-flag-to-chute-flag ~368 ft, chute-to-Hill-3-start ~568 ft), but these are internal measurements, not official [drive:srs/Raceday '24|R2].

**Driving the chute.** SRS driver training teaches three chute approaches — enter tight / let out wide / exit tight (a "parenthesis" line, smoothest but can drag); curve wide at the flag then enter straight (most controlled); and enter-center-stay-center (safest, used to train new drivers) [drive:srs/driver training|R2]. Chute behavior is buggy-specific: SRS computed critical (oversteer-divergence) speeds of ~20 mph for Inviscid versus ~27 mph for Seraph, and since the team turns near 27 mph, Inviscid's low critical speed "explains why it spins so easily" [wiki:research:buggy_vehicle_dynamics|R2]. "Fishtailing" — the rear sliding during the turn — is generic technique despite the "SDC fishtail" nickname [discord:sdc/#general|R3].

**Speeds are coarse.** Representative freeroll benchmarks (all team-collected, stopwatch/GPS-based, incomplete and noisy — never cite a single run): a Nov 2016 comparison had SDC's Malice at ~53.7 s / ~37 mph, PiKA's Banshee ~55.3 s / ~35 mph, and Spirit's Inviscid ~57.6 s / ~34 mph; women's freeroll top speed is estimated ~32 mph versus men's ~36 mph [see topics/course.md][discord:baa/#low-sodium-alumni|R3]. Deteriorating pavement measurably slows the course — the 2015 raceday's fastest freeroll (53.5 s) was slower than five 2011 buggies that broke 53 s — which is why paving milestones historically raised record hopes [news:2015/04|R3].

**Landmarks and navigation.** Because the driver sits only about **3 inches off the ground** and cannot see over the road's crown, driving is done by fixed visual markers and by **flaggers**. Key markers: the start, the Hill-2 stop sign (by convention "the stop sign" always means the one on Schenley), the Phipps crosswalk, the Bigelow monument (drivers pass to its **left** — going right is a classic wrong-way error), the **transition flag** near the crosswalk (tells the driver where to aim over a blind crest), and the **chute flag** (tells the driver when to begin the turn) [web:reference/course|R2][pod:s01e04|R3]. The chute is lined by roughly **300 hay bales each fall and spring, two layers deep on both sides** — hay, not straw, per the bylaws, "because it holds up better for repeated use" (one podcast source says straw, on cost grounds; surfaced, not resolved) [drive:srs/Bylaws|R2][pod:s06e01|R3].

**The course changes.** Over a century the route has moved and been repaved many times (paving milestones include the chute in 1995, the back hills in 2014–15, and Hill 1 in 2025), and recent construction has narrowed operations: the Highmark Wellness Center (on the old Skibo Gym site) killed **Lane 3** and cut Raceday to **two lanes since 2022**, a 2023 Frew redesign made Frew one-way with bollards, and the Panther Hollow bridge closed in late 2024 [see topics/course.md]. The consensus rule for records is that as long as the course still runs Tech → Schenley → Frew over the same five hills, it counts as the "same course" [discord:baa/#rumor-mill|R3].

## 3. The vehicle

A buggy is a **composite monocoque**: the shell is simultaneously the body and the structural chassis [drive:srs/All About Buggies|R2]. It is unmotorized and, in the human classes, carries one driver lying nearly flat, steering and braking by hand. Required components per the rules and SRS documentation are the **shell, pushbar, steering (which includes braking), wheels, windscreen, and debris shield**; the buggy must protect the driver's eyes, hands, mouth, and head, and fit within a maximum footprint of **15 ft × 6 ft** including the pushbar [drive:srs/All About Buggies|R2][web:official-rules|R1].

**Anatomy.** The **shell/monocoque** is the composite body and roll structure. The **pushbar** is the rear bar the pushers contact; it transfers their force into the buggy, and flaggers raise flags late so a driver does not hit an obscured bar. **Fairings** are aerodynamic covers over the wheels (they can come loose and become a hazard). The **windscreen** is clear Lexan for debris protection; a **debris shield** is required if a wheel sits ahead of the driver. The **harness** restrains the driver and is tilt- and pull-tested. **Steering** is at the front or rear depending on layout, and integrates the **brakes**. Underneath are the **axles, bearings, and wheels**, prepped weekly — SRS frames the wheels and axle as effectively the suspension and "what makes us truly fast" [drive:srs/First Mech Meeting|R3].

**Two classes of design (layout).** Buggies come in two chassis geometries [web:reference/buggy-the-vehicle|R2]:

- **Standard / Forward Trike** — one front wheel, two rear (SRS, CIA, PiKA, SAE, SDC historically). Simple direct steering; believed more stable in the push but more prone to spin in the chute.
- **Reverse Trike** — two front wheels, one rear (Fringe, SigEp, SigNu, AEPi, and some others). Uses **wagon steering** or **Ackermann steering**; better driver ergonomics and a sealed compartment, but more complex.

Historically the two were nicknamed "Legend-type" (standard) and "Jama-type" (reverse) after early exemplars [news:2020/02|R2]. Because the course is raced in only one direction with a fixed right-hand chute, teams tune buggies **asymmetrically** for that turn [drive:srs/SRS Mechanics|R2].

**Materials.** Shells are **carbon-fiber** (CFRP) and **kevlar** composites, sometimes with glass fiber, built by hand layup over a mold or plug. Composites vocabulary an engineer will meet: **layup** (building a part from fiber and resin), **mold/plug**, **peel ply**, **vacuum bag**, and the fiber types themselves [drive:srs/Engineering Composites|R2]. This overview treats materials at a summary level; the composites-manufacturing and shell-design-aero topic files carry the CFD, layup schedules, and structural detail.

**Wheels and the heating practice (important disambiguation).** In buggy usage the **wheel** is the hub the tire sits on and the **tire** is the thermoplastic-polyurethane (TPU) band around it, though informally the whole assembly is "the wheel" [wiki:mvp:anatomy|R2]. Wheels, bearings, and axles are prepped weekly, and because rolling resistance dominates much of a run, wheel choice and preparation are treated as a primary speed lever — SRS calls the wheels "what makes us truly fast" [drive:srs/First Mech Meeting|R3]. A practice that constantly confuses extractors: wheels/tires are commonly **pre-warmed for grip** before racing, typically toward **~150°F**, via a heater and controller; "0" means unheated. **In build and data contexts, "heat" means this tire heating, not ambient weather or a race heat** [drive:srs/Heating documentation|R2][data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3]. A repeatedly documented failure mode is a **surface-vs-core discrepancy**: teams record the correct surface temperature while the core never actually reaches target, so "heated to 150°F" can be optimistic — thermal models suggest stagnant-air heating needs about 3 hours to bring a wheel to 150°F, far longer than a typical pre-roll warm-up [drive:srs/Raceday '24|R3][wiki:components:wheels|R2]. Note the COVID-era precedent (§7) that banning wheel heating is a deliberate safety-driven speed penalty. Wheel-set names are a loose categorical (Halos, Diablos, 21s, 22s, "Mixed"); wheel compound and hardness are tracked but the databases are sparse, and multiple teams have built bench rigs (spin-down drums, bounce tests, thermal ANSYS models) to compare compounds. **Treat any single wheel-test or heating number as bench-scale and not race-validated.**

**Aerodynamics and stability.** Fairings and shell shape matter at freeroll speeds, and fairings also reduce wheel heat loss on-road (a fairing-enclosed wheel stays measurably warmer than a bare one) [wiki:components:wheels|R2]. Center of gravity and cornering stiffness govern chute behavior; with no vendor data, SRS's vehicle-dynamics analysis assumes cornering stiffness ≈ 16.5% of wheel load [drive:srs/SRS Mechanics|R2]. This overview stays at the summary level; the shell-design-aero, wheels, and steering-brakes-hardware topic files carry the CFD, compound data, and structural analysis.

## 4. People & roles

A **Team** is one driver plus five pushers; an **entry** is one buggy, one team, and one full **alternate team** (a substitute driver and five substitute pushers) [web:official-rules|R1]. Beyond team, roles divide into a small set an agent should recognize:

- **Pushers** — the five who run and shove the buggy uphill, each responsible for one hill. Pushing is a sprint discipline: every hill is under 200 m, so teams train fast-twitch power, not distance [discord:sdc/#pushers|R3].
- **Driver** — the (deliberately small and light) person who rides inside the buggy and steers, especially through the freeroll and chute. Drivers are heavily regulated for safety (see §5) and qualify to a **specific driver-and-buggy pairing**.
- **Mechanics** — the members who design, build, and prep the buggy. Teams have their own names for them: SigNu calls theirs "Chickens," CIA historically "gnomes" [discord:baa/#general|R3].
- **Flaggers** — hold the **transition** and **chute** flags as visual landmarks for the low-seated driver, and the **stop flag** for hazards. Every org provides a chute flagger per driver; if any flagger throws a stop flag, all must [drive:robobuggy/Bylaws|R2].
- **Sweeps / chore crew** — clear leaves, debris, and roadkill before rolls, lay and remove hay bales, and guard the **barricades/bollards** that keep cars off the course. "No bales, no buggy" is a standing rule [discord:sdc/announcements|R3].
- **Follow car / follow truck** — a vehicle trailing the last buggy for incident response; on Raceday a Sweepstakes-operated **follow truck** trails each heat, and it must carry driver-extraction tools [web:reference/teams|R2].
- **Chairs** — each team has leadership (chairman and assistant chairman, plus heads/leads for mechanics, pushers, drivers, data, and so on). SRS calls its subteams **sectors** and its officer/leadership group the **"Kru"** — note that "Kru" means the officers, not the whole membership [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3].

Operationally, a team's year is a build-and-practice cycle: mechanics design and fabricate (or refurbish) buggies through the fall and winter, drivers accumulate qualifying rolls across the fall and spring practice seasons, pushers train through midnights, and the whole organization converges on the Spring Carnival Raceday. Roles overlap heavily on small teams, and members often cross between sectors.

**The Sweepstakes committee** is the student body that runs the race. It is built around three elected officials — **Chairman** (presiding), **Assistant Chairman** ("Aschir"), and **Safety Chairman** — plus appointed Design and Buggy Book chairmen and one Buggy Chairman per participating org; each org holds one vote and a two-thirds quorum is needed to act [web:official-rules|R1]. A CMU staff **Advisor** holds broad discretionary authority over the organization and rules. For 2025–26 the committee added Finance, Assistant Safety, Mechanical Safety, and an elected Operations Chair, and flagged the Operations and Spring Safety chairs as "core" roles without which rolls cannot run [news:2025/05|R2].

**Alternate substitution** follows a strict direction: an alternate-team pusher may substitute laterally or *downward* only — a B-team pusher may fill in for an A-team pusher, not the reverse [drive:srs/RD22 PUSHERS|R2]. Cross-team coordination during rolls and Raceday runs over a **Sweeps** Discord and the **Zello** walkie-talkie app, and the **CMU Radio Club (W3VC)** staffs a safety net during freerolls, calling buggy positions (e.g. "top of hill," "Panther Hollow bridge," "chute") so the course is watched end to end [discord:sweeps/#general|R3][pod:s02e05|R3].

**The BAA — Buggy Alumni Association** — was founded in **2008**. It preserves the sport's history and publishes **cmubuggy.org**, which is the origin of essentially every `web:` source in this corpus, including the official race results that canon.md is computed from [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3]. The BAA also maintains historical timing references, funds telemetry and safety projects through an endowment, and runs Truck Weekend timing to seed safe Raceday heats. When this overview says a result is "official," it ultimately traces to a cmubuggy.org table curated by the BAA.

## 5. Rules essentials

CMU Buggy is governed by a single living document, the **"Rules, Regulations, and Procedures"** (the Bylaws), maintained as LaTeX in the public repo **github.com/CMUBuggy/rules** and treated as authoritative by cmubuggy.org [web:official-rules|R1]. One caveat for anyone treating the repo as history: from roughly 2015 to 2022 changes were made but **not committed to GitHub**, and public pull requests only resumed in 2023, so the repo is authoritative for current rule *text* but is **not a reliable change history** [see topics/rules-safety.md]. The current season may also race under approved-but-uncommitted updates.

**Classes and entries.** The four classes are Men's, Women's, All Gender (rules in a yearly-revised appendix, since ~2023), and Robotic (driverless, codified by late 2022) [web:official-rules|R1]. Each org may field up to four entries per class (one for All Gender), adding at most one new team per year without a chairmen's vote, and entries within a class must run fastest-to-slowest as A/B/C/D — violating that order DQs the *faster* entry, though this "rank DQ" is rarely enforced [web:official-rules|R1].

**Driver qualification — three distinct gates, routinely confused.** An agent must keep these separate:

- **Capes ("Capabilities Tests")** — a per-semester, per-driver-and-buggy equipment and safety inspection (field-of-vision, brake, "snake," and pull tests, plus a full safety inspection). A buggy cannot roll until it passes Capes [web:reference/safety|R2].
- **Drops (braking tests)** — a **drop test** is required before **every** freeroll and **after** every race (so brakes must survive the whole race — "one-shot" brake designs are impossible): released from rest, the buggy must roll a 30-ft zone and stop within 15 ft. A separate braking-capability test checks stopping from speed [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Pass Test** — a specific **driving-safety maneuver**, introduced in 1973: the driver, in the specific raceday buggy, must safely **overtake a slower freerolling buggy** on a defined stretch of the course, judged from the follow car. It is **not** a generic pass/fail exam, and it attaches to a **specific driver-and-buggy combination** — switch to a wholly new buggy and you requalify from scratch [web:official-rules|R1][pod:s03e05|R3].

Beyond these, drivers must accumulate qualifying rolls (current rules: **new drivers ≥15 total rolls, returning ≥10**, with capped fall-to-spring carryover), and **bagging** (a drag device on the pushbar to slow the buggy) is required on a driver's first roll each semester, a new driver's first several rolls, and the first roll after any accident [web:official-rules|R1].

**Driver PPE — the "5 Points of Safety"** [web:official-rules|R1]: (1) **helmet** (ANSI Z90.4 / SNELL bicycle or CSA hockey), (2) **goggles** (ANSI Z87.1), (3) custom-fitted **mouthguard** (ANSI/ADA 99), (4) full **leather gloves**, and (5) a **harness** of at least three attachment points, straps ≥1.75" wide, tool-free release, UIAA-certified clips, and professional box-stitching. The protective **cage** must resist 1,000 lbf frontally on a 1×72" bar, 500 lbf on the sides/rear, and a ~408 lb top plate without deforming onto the driver — colloquially the **"pencil test"** (no gap larger than a pencil, and an "infinitely stiff rod" must never reach the driver). The windscreen must be clear Lexan ≥0.062" mechanically fastened along more than two edges so it fails before its supports [web:official-rules|R1]. A persistent, documented gap: the rulebook sets **no minimum harness-attachment strength**, and the recurring real-world failure is the harness *attachment points tearing out of the shell*, not the harness webbing itself. This is not hypothetical: in the 2014 Fringe "Banyan" crash all three harness attachment points failed on a >35 mph curb hit, and Rachael Schmitt's 2015 crash testing found a rules-compliant, properly-harnessed dummy fully ejected because the attachment points to the buggy failed. Her EMS-records review estimated a ~1% incident rate, with severe injuries almost always to the cervical spine [see topics/rules-safety.md].

**Course-side and operational safety.** A raceday **follow car** stays ~100 ft behind each heat and must carry driver-extraction tools (missing = fine plus DQ); a freeroll follow car must be within 15 seconds of the last buggy. Fire safety is strict after the 1986 ATO fire: prep areas need ≥2 fire extinguishers, combustible liquids (beyond ≤2 fl oz bearing lube) and open flame are banned, and violations draw heavy fines plus a multi-month org ban [web:official-rules|R1][see topics/rules-safety.md]. A minimum-temperature cutoff exists for cancelling cold rolls but its exact value drifts across editions (variously 32°F, 26°F, a 28°F "feels-like"), so treat it as low-confidence and driver-judgment-dependent [see topics/rules-safety.md].

**DQ triggers.** The official results-table DQ types are **Lane, Pushbar, 5 Second, Fire Safety, Follow Truck, Loss of Mass, and Drops** [web:toptimes|R1]. Longstanding automatic-DQ conditions include all four wheels outside the lane, **loss of mass** (losing any shell/hatch/cover part mid-run), the **5-second rule** (only the driver, Hill-1 pushers, and starter within 5 ft once the countdown hits 5), pacing, transition-zone violations, false starts, a missing pushbar-hand at the finish, and fire-safety violations [web:official-rules|R1]. Canon records **619 DQ rows** across all history [canon|R1]. A DQ'd run keeps its recorded time but earns no placing.

**The 2026 overhaul.** Effective 2026, many previously-automatic *non-safety* DQs became **stacking 6-second time penalties** (three penalties in one heat = automatic DQ), while **loss of mass** and the **finish-line pushbar rule** were explicitly kept as full DQs. For example, a person within 5 ft at the "5" count now draws a penalty but only a person still touching at "go" is a DQ; one wheel out of lane is a penalty, two or more still a DQ [see topics/rules-safety.md][discord:baa/general 2026|R3]. This was prompted by a 2025 SigEp pacing DQ and a 2023 CIA lane DQ that each erased strong finishes on non-interfering technicalities.

The modern safety regime was forged by specific disasters — a **1971** DTD paralysis created the Safety Chair role and drove the 1972 overhaul plus the 1973 Pass Test; a **1986** ATO truck fire triggered a rulebook rewrite (from roughly 10 to 80–100 pages) that added hockey helmets, roll-cage load requirements, and fire rules [see topics/rules-safety.md]. For enforcement lore, incident case studies, and the full DQ taxonomy, see corpus/topics/rules-safety.md and incidents-safety-catalog.md.

## 6. Teams landscape

Eleven organizations race in the modern era; about half are Greek (fraternities and sororities) and half are independent (non-Greek) [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3]. A recruiting rule of thumb heard in the community: "join SDC if you want to win, CIA if you want to have fun, Fringe for a bit of both" [pod:s02e06|R3]. One paragraph each:

- **SDC (Student Dormitory Council)** — independent; the dominant modern team, holder of the top course times, with sin-themed buggies (Malice, Inferno, Bane, Paranoia, Gluttony, Lust, Vice, Havoc). SDC has 30 championships and the all-time Men's and Women's records, both set by Malice in 2017 [canon|R1][web:org/SDC|R2]. It was also the subject of the corpus's clearest org-level enforcement episode: a 2023 Sweepstakes investigation barred SDC from Raceday proper (permitting a restricted exhibition) after ruling it had trained drivers to circumvent the capes vision test [see topics/rules-safety.md].
- **CIA (Carnegie Involvement Association)** — independent; recent bird-themed buggies (Starling, Goldfinch, Roadrunner, Kingfisher, Emperor, Equinox) and famous older ones (Firebird, Freyja). CIA swept Men's championships 2024–2026 and fields the most data-gathering tech, with five buggies rolling in Fall 2025 [canon|R1][news:2025/12|R2].
- **PiKA (Pi Kappa Alpha)** — fraternity; the winningest org in history with **40 championships**, buggies including Raptor, Banshee, Chimera, and Zeus [canon|R1].
- **Apex (APX)** — independent, the spiritual successor to the defunct Pioneers; buggies Kerosene, Helios, Scorch, Nova, Solaris, Firefly, and Kelvin (autonomous). Apex keeps the cross-org **raceday timing sheets** used across this corpus and won the first All Gender championship (2023) [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3][canon|R1].
- **Fringe (FRI)** — independent; nearly all buggy names start with B (Balsa, Beetle, Bermuda, Blackbird, Bonsai, Banyan, Blueshift). Seven championships [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3][canon|R1].
- **SigEp, SAE, SigNu** — fraternities; **DG (Delta Gamma)** — sorority. These round out the human-pushed field alongside the independents above. A note on eligibility that shapes the landscape: fraternities and sororities face pusher-recruitment restrictions that independents do not, a legacy of an era of Greek (especially PiKA) dominance; Women's buggy has never had these restrictions [see topics/rules-safety.md].
- **SRS / Spirit / SPI (Spirit Racing Systems)** — the corpus's focal team, treated more fully below.
- **Robobuggy (RBO)** — the independent autonomous team, treated below.

**SRS / Spirit / SPI.** "Spirit," "SPI," and "SRS" all refer to the same independent (non-Greek) team, **Spirit Racing Systems**, which is a facet of SPIRIT, CMU's Black Student Union, and operates as a multicultural org within it [news:2020/02|R2]. Its **founding year conflicts** in the sources — SRS slides say "Fall of 1984" while a team trivia packet implies 1985; the corpus does not resolve it, so treat the founding as "mid-1980s" [see topics/srs-team.md]. Spirit's competitive **peak was the late 1980s–1990s** (Quantum Leap ran a 2:06.20 Men's course record in 1988, and Spirit took multiple Men's and Women's titles 1987–1998), and it holds **17 championships** all-time [canon|R1]. Its buggies carry evocative and Swahili names — Seraph, Inviscid, Kingpin / Kingpin II, Mapambazuko ("Zuke"), Shaka Zulu, Haraka, Menes, Zenith, Demani — and its continuously-tracked 2024–26 fleet is **Seraph, Zuke, Inviscid, Kingpin II** [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3][data:summary_stats|R3]. SRS subteams are **sectors** (Mechs, Pushers, Data, Flaggers, Drivers, Mechanical Design); the officer group is the **"Kru"** (not the whole team); and much SRS branding — the secret motto "NFJ," the year-varying vision acronyms (BUILD, later RISE) — is team-internal lore best treated as R3 [see topics/srs-team.md].

**Robobuggy (the independent autonomous team).** Robobuggy is the driverless team: its buggies (NAND, Short Circuit, Transistor, Singularity) are autonomous or teleoperated and carry no human. It has won every Robotic championship 2023–2026 and holds the Robotic course record (**3:09.70**, 2018, NAND) [canon|R1]. Robotic buggies are a materially different regime: they may store internal energy only to run onboard systems, **never to add forward kinetic energy** (which is why servos and retractable pushbars are legal), and they are very light — light enough that they may need a push in the freeroll where a human buggy would coast [see topics/rules-safety.md][see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §3]. Robotic capes and rules differ accordingly (battery-failure plans, a manual-override demo, an on-course e-stop test), robotic orgs vote but do not count toward freeroll quorum, and robotic buggies get exclusive course time before the roll order [see topics/rules-safety.md]. Robobuggy also drives the community's **RTK GPS** telemetry effort (see §8) and runs the corpus's most extensive autonomy stack (GPS + IMU + wheel encoders fused through Kalman-family filters across a decade) [see topics/data-telemetry.md]. For an agent, the key point is that statements about robotic buggies behaving oddly — needing a push, different speeds, unusual sensors — are normal for the class, not anomalies.

## 7. History in brief, with records

Buggy began in **1920**, reportedly from fraternity members racing wheeled rain barrels down Flagstaff Hill, and has run every Spring Carnival since except the WWII years (1942–1945) and 2020 (COVID) [web:reference/overview|R2][canon|R1]. The 1920 rulebook had just five rules (four wheels, a two-person crew, a weight minimum, a mandatory wheel-swap pit stop, and one crew member's feet always on the ground). The four-wheel rule fell in **1932**, when Beta debuted the first three-wheeler; **1933** brought the first aerodynamic breakthrough (an airplane-silk-cloaked three-wheeler, the theorized origin of the name "buggy," resembling a baby buggy's sun-hood) [see topics/history.md]. **Women's** racing began in **1979** and the **All Gender** division in **2023** [canon|R1].

Other structural firsts shaped the modern race: the course settled into its current Tech → Schenley → Frew layout when **Frew Street was completed in 1928** (which also opened Buggy beyond the Greek system to men's dormitory teams), mass races gave way to grouped **heats** in the late 1920s, and starting **lanes** were added in the late 1930s after teams boxed rivals into obstacles [see topics/course.md]. The centennial ("Buggy100," slated for 2020) was displaced by COVID and celebrated at Spring Carnival 2022 [see topics/sport-overview.md].

The sport's competitive eras, from the record progressions: fraternities dominated the early-to-mid century (KapSig, Beta, ATO, DTD), PiKA built the largest championship total across many decades, Spirit peaked in the late 1980s–1990s, and **SDC has dominated the modern era** (a 10-edition Men's championship streak 2012–2022, bridging the COVID gap) [canon|R1]. Spirit's own record touchpoints from canon illustrate its peak: Men's 2:06.20 (1988, Quantum Leap), and a Women's progression from 2:38.94 (1986, Elan) down to 2:33.03 (1995, Shaka Zulu) [canon|R1]. The COVID period was exceptional: **2020 was fully cancelled** — the first cancellation since WWII — and **2021 was limited** to a prelim-only, "better of two rolls" format with **no finals and no wheel heating** — a precedent that banning wheel treatment can be a deliberate safety-driven speed penalty [canon|R1][see topics/rules-safety.md].

## 8. Data & terminology for engineers

An agent working on buggy software will encounter team-collected telemetry, and the single most important thing to internalize is that **it is incomplete and noisy, for coarse use only**. Essentially every number outside canon.md comes from hand-triggered, ad-hoc student sensors and years of manual entry. Key columns are **23–56% empty**, and outlier maxima run **3–6× the p90** and are almost certainly sensor or stopwatch noise, not records [data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3][see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §5]. The governing rule is absolute: **never cite a single row** — one roll's max speed, one push time, one date's weather — as fact. Only cmubuggy.org official results (canon.md) reach **R1**, and none of that lives in the telemetry pipeline.

**The srs.db / RollVis provenance (backfilled, not independent).** SRS's data has three generations: (1) **RollVisAnalytics**, a MATLAB pipeline (2017–2024) that ingested Garmin VIRB action-camera GPX exports and maintained `wheel_db.xlsx`, `pusher_db.xlsx`, and `freeroll_db.xlsx`; (2) **srs.db and the "SRS" web app** (a FastAPI + React + SQLite system built during the RD26 cycle, 2026); and (3) a planned RD27 custom RTK-GPS sensor [data:tooling_overview|R2]. The load-bearing caveat: **`srs.db` was created in the RD26 cycle and backfilled with transformed RollVisAnalytics + Google Drive data**, so pre-2026 rows are *derived* from those older sources. **`srs.db` agreeing with RollVis is therefore NOT independent corroboration, and disagreements are transformation artifacts as often as data errors** [data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3][see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §5]. Do not treat the two as separate witnesses.

**The three speed-unit regimes (never merge or compare across them).** Speed columns use three different, undocumented units [data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3]:

| Source | Column | Presumed unit | Typical values |
|---|---|---|---|
| `srs.db` / CSV exports | `Max Speed` | m/s (inferred) | cluster ~10–16 |
| RollVis `freeroll_db.xlsx` | `MaxSpeed` | km/h (inferred, ~3×) | median ~30 |
| `rolls_times` crosswalk | speeds | mph | — |

The units are **inferred, not documented**; treating them as one scale is a guaranteed error. Additional structural facts: the VIRB GPS records at only **1 Hz** (so roll timing is accurate only to the nearest second), the orientation channels (pitch, roll, azimuth) in per-roll exports were **never populated** (logged as constant 0), `pusher.gender` and `rolldate.temperature`/`humidity` are **100% null**, there are **no 2020 rows** (COVID), and **every row is `weekend`-type — no official raceday data lives in this pipeline** [data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3][wiki:data:roll_visualization|R2]. Concrete illustrations of the noise: in one archive, 38.8% of non-null Max Speed values are below 1.0 and must be read as "no usable reading," and the single highest recorded Max Speed (21.37) is a clear outlier versus the next cluster (15.8–16.8) and is flagged as sensor noise, not a top speed [data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3]. Push-time medians (Hill 1 ~22.4 s, Hill 2 ~11.7 s, Hill 3 ~15.0 s, Hill 4 ~29.2 s, Hill 5 ~33.2 s) are usable as shape, but every hill's *maximum* recorded push time is 3–6× its p90 and is timing noise (a dropped pusher, a mistimed stopwatch), never a record [data:DATA_DICTIONARY|R3]. Even survey coordinates are soft: the corpus's course geometry comes from student handheld-GPS "course walks," meter-scale and copy-pasted in places (one landmark shares exact coordinates with another, and there is no recorded Hill-5 end), not survey-grade data [see topics/course.md].

Community RTK-GPS efforts (Robobuggy, CIA, Apex, and SRS's later RaceBox loggers) are more accurate (single-digit cm for RTK) but were still nascent for SRS as of 2026, and even RaceBox is ~30 cm at best and roughly 10× worse at low pusher speeds [see topics/data-telemetry.md]. The RTK ecosystem is anchored by a **base station on the Hamerschlag Hall smokestack**, surveyed to millimeter accuracy and built by Robobuggy with the CMU Radio Club for roughly $1,600–1,750 [see topics/data-telemetry.md]. Other teams run parallel programs worth knowing about: **Apex** keeps cross-org raceday time sheets and a regression finding that Hill 1+2 pusher speed explains about half of freeroll-time variance (driver and buggy the other half); **CIA** fields the most data tech (five buggies, RTK, an RShiny "Pusherboard"); and **SDC** runs a drone OpenCV driving-line pipeline and built its own harness load-test rig [see topics/data-telemetry.md][news:2025/10|R2]. All of it is candidly self-described as incomplete — SRS dashboards carry warnings like "some of these numbers are sus" — which is itself the strongest evidence for the coarse-only rule [drive:srs/Raceday '23|R2].

**Official-timing lineage (separate, and the only R1 telemetry).** Official raceday times come from stopwatches, then a pre-2010 "magic box," then the **FinishLynx** photo-finish system (2010–), and today a Phoenix Sports Technology vendor plus a broadcast speed trap — routed through the BAA to cmubuggy.org, and thence to canon.md [see topics/data-telemetry.md]. Even this drifts: in 2026 a chute camera ran ~5 s fast for ~25 minutes and displayed roll times had to be corrected by hand. Those corrected numbers, and every radar-gun and split figure in the corpus, are approximate — **official finish order and times are R1 from canon.md only**.

### Key glossary (~30 terms)

- **Buggy / Sweepstakes** — the sport; the unmotorized relay-raced vehicle and the race itself [web:home|R2].
- **Pusher** — one of five per team who run and shove the buggy uphill, one per hill [news:2024/09|R2].
- **Driver** — the small person who rides inside the buggy and steers, especially through freeroll and chute [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §1].
- **Mechanic** — team member who builds and preps the buggy (SigNu "Chickens," CIA "gnomes") [discord:baa/#general|R3].
- **Flagger** — holds transition/chute/stop flags as landmarks and hazard signals for the low-seated driver [web:reference/teams|R2].
- **Sweeps / chores** — clearing debris and laying/removing hay bales and bollards before rolls [drive:srs/Fall 2020 Rolls|R3].
- **Roll / Freeroll (practice)** — one completion of the course; weekend dawn practices, sunrise–9am [web:reference/freerolls|R2].
- **Midnights / Push Practice** — late-night, hill-specific pusher-only practice sessions [drive:srs/RD22 Seminar|R3].
- **Capes (Capabilities Tests)** — per-semester harness/brake/vision safety checks before a buggy may roll [web:reference/safety|R2].
- **Drops (drop test)** — braking test required before every freeroll and after every race [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Pass Test** — driver-qualification safe-pass maneuver, not a generic exam; per driver-and-buggy pair (since 1973) [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Hills 1–5 / front hills (1–2) / back hills (3–5)** — the five pushed segments [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §1].
- **Freeroll (segment)** — the un-pushed downhill portion of the course [web:reference/course|R2].
- **Chute** — the sharp right turn at the bottom of the freeroll (Schenley → Frew); the main crash site [web:reference/course|R2].
- **Rollout / Window / Window 0** — coasting distance past the chute, measured informally in Porter Hall "windows"; Window 0 = Porter/Scaife driveway edge [web:reference/course|R2].
- **Transition zone** — a 45-ft area between hills where two pushers may both touch the buggy [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Pickup** — the Hill 3 pusher matching the buggy's speed as it rolls uphill again [news:2024/09|R2].
- **King/Queen/Monarch of the Hill (Blinn/Brewer)** — the fastest Hill 1 pusher of the raceday, by class [pod:s06e04|R3].
- **Heat** — triple-ambiguous: a grouped race of several teams; tire/wheel heating; or the RollVis `Heat` *column* (= tire temperature, not weather) [see pipeline/qa/domain_primer.md §6].
- **Entry / Team / Alternate Team** — one buggy + a team (1 driver, 5 pushers) + a full alternate team [web:official-rules|R1].
- **A/B/C/D team** — an org's entries within a class, A most competitive; must race fastest-to-slowest [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Standard/Forward Trike vs Reverse Trike** — 1-front/2-rear vs 2-front/1-rear layouts (a.k.a. Legend-type vs Jama-type) [web:reference/buggy-the-vehicle|R2].
- **Monocoque / shell** — the composite body that is also the chassis [drive:srs/All About Buggies|R2].
- **Pushbar** — the rear bar the pushers contact; a missing pushbar-hand at the finish is a DQ [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Fairings** — aerodynamic covers over the wheels; can come loose and become a hazard [slack:cia/#build|R3].
- **Harness / pencil test** — driver restraint (≥3 points); the cage "pencil test" requires no gap larger than a pencil could reach the driver [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Wheel vs tire** — the hub vs the TPU band around it; the whole assembly is informally "the wheel" [wiki:mvp:anatomy|R2].
- **Heating / ~150°F** — pre-race warming of wheels/tires for grip (0 = unheated); "heat" in build/data contexts, not weather [drive:srs/Heating documentation|R2].
- **Loss of mass** — losing any shell/hatch/cover part mid-run; a full DQ even after the 2026 overhaul [web:official-rules|R1].
- **Stop flag** — the only yellow flag (black X); means "controlled stop, hazard ahead"; if one flagger throws it, all must [web:reference/safety|R2].
- **Bagging** — attaching a drag device to the pushbar to slow a buggy for new-driver and post-accident rolls [web:official-rules|R1].
- **RTK GPS / turn loss** — real-time-kinematic GPS (cm-accurate) is the community's telemetry ambition; "turn loss" = chute-exit speed ÷ chute-entry speed, the standard chute-performance metric [see topics/data-telemetry.md].

**Bottom line for an engineer.** Treat canon.md as the only authoritative source of results and records; treat the rulebook (github.com/CMUBuggy/rules) as authoritative for current rule text but not history; and treat every team-collected number as coarse, unit-ambiguous, and non-independent unless proven otherwise. Quote the trend, never the single row. For the full data model, unit analysis, sensor generations, and per-team programs, see corpus/topics/data-telemetry.md; for how the whole corpus was assembled and weighted, see corpus/research_context.md.
